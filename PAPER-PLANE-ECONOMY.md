# Paper-plane rewards

## What one ad buys

**Guests.** “Watch one ad to unlock the advanced report for this domain for 24 hours.” Open the report as soon as completion is verified, in the same guest browser, without registration. The window starts at grant and includes later published scans of that domain. Offer the ad only when advanced data and eligible inventory exist. The free report remains available after a decline or no fill. Domain access can be exercised locally with Google's development sample. Production settlement requires an authenticated provider receipt bound to the pending offer and guest.

**Local account reward experiment.** The main “Watch an ad to unlock” action opens the report immediately on completion. A secondary reward option lets a guest defer redemption. Show the guaranteed unlock, the account requirement for a flight, its odds and its possible zero payout before opt-in. On completion, show “Open report for 24 hours” as the primary action and “Create an account to try a flight” as the secondary action on the same screen. Keep the guaranteed claim available if signup is cancelled. Redeem the claim atomically for exactly one benefit. Once the guest report is open, a dismissible invitation can say “Want a chance at more? Create an account to try a flight with your next eligible ad.” Ignoring it leaves the open report and its expiry alone. Each ad has one promised reward: direct access, account points, a flight, or one explicitly offered choice ticket. A signed-in visitor chooses between the domain unlock and **one flight for 0–500 points**; there is no flat-points option, because 50 points would only buy the same unlock. Signed-in visitors can also earn flights without a report on the points page (Account → Get more points): each fully watched ad gives exactly one flight, and a closed or failed ad gives nothing. Choosing a flight uses that ad's entire reward, including when the payout is zero. Earnings from previous flights remain in the wallet. There is no entry fee or stake. The local worker and website support flight settlement and the points wallet with development sample ads. Production issuance remains disabled.

Test **50 points per domain unlock** and **50 points per eligible refresh**. One guaranteed award would buy either action, and unused points carry forward. Show refresh eligibility before the ad and reserve any refresh explicitly promised. An existing entitlement, unavailable advanced report, in-progress scan or cooldown incurs no new debit. If a charged refresh ends in terminal failure, reverse exactly the original debit once in the same wallet. Key the reversal to that debit so a retry or duplicate failure notice cannot credit it twice. Keep the existing free scan allocation and per-domain limits. The paid API already refunds its own failed charged scans in API credits. Pro access and API credits remain independent of these development points.

The proposed volunteer pilot would award **25 points per verified contribution**, half the price of one action. Raising the offer to **30** would require validated savings after verification and review. Earnings from voluntary studies and account ad rewards could be spent from the same points balance. ## What the handoff can tell us

The three selected flights ended at **3, 93 and 236**. The median endpoint was **93**, while the amounts actually banked were **0, 90 and 81**, with a median payout of **81**. The original rewards were seconds removed from a wait. For scale, a DataDeal flight paying 81 points would cover one 50-point action and leave 31 points. Three selected examples cannot predict a typical DataDeal payout. A separate mention of 31 in the handoff has no confirmed outcome type. The earlier uniform model had 360 losing thresholds plus one cap win. Under that assumption collecting at 90 succeeds on 271/361 draws and yields about 67.6 units per attempt. It was a hypothetical pilot, and its three-attempt variant changes those odds again.

The timing CSV supports an accelerating offer of approximately `exp(0.59085 × active flight seconds)`. The recording pauses at 36; DataDeal pauses at 50, exactly one report unlock. After taking 81 the player sees the receipt immediately while the animation continues to 236. Preserve those distinct banked and final amounts. For DataDeal display points, keep a single draw through pauses, and allow an automatic bank target before launch for keyboard, mobile and reduced-motion users.

## Local development balance

Use a single continuous offer with a maximum of **500** points. The hidden end point is uniform, so a flight is still in the air at integer `n` with probability `1 − (n − 1) / 500`: failing before 20 happens about 4% of the time, before 50 about 10%, and the 500 cap is reached (and auto-banked) once in 500 flights. The median maximum is 250.

**Example.** For one report today, take the guaranteed unlock or bank 50 at the checkpoint (90% of flights get there). A player aiming for two reports banks at 100 and succeeds about 80 times in 100. From the 50-point checkpoint, a plane still in the air reaches 100 about 89% of the time. Continuing and missing adds zero. Previous earnings are always retained.

For reproducibility, draw one uniform `U` in (0, 1), then set `T = 1 + 200 × (1 / sqrt(U) − 1)`. The uncapped flight ends at continuous offer T. A target succeeds strictly before T. If T exceeds 1,000 the server automatically banks 1,000 at the cap. No collect can exceed the floored live offer. A simulation with 100,000 draws reached 36 / 50 / 100 / 200 / 500 / 1,000 on 72,498 / 64,634 / 44,698 / 25,034 / 8,128 / 2,722 flights. Its median maximum reachable offer was 83. To repeat it, initialize `s = 20261125`, advance `s = (1664525 × s + 1013904223) mod 2³²` per draw, and use `U = (s + 0.5) / 2³²`. The local pilot uses cryptographic randomness and a server-authoritative clock. These illustrative odds assume no endpoint clues and exclude manual timing and network delay. The timing beyond the recorded endpoint is extrapolated.

## Cost and limits

The largest expected payout of any fixed target is approximately 50.25 points per completed ad, at 199 points. That is close to the guaranteed 50. The maximum liability on an individual flight is 1,000 points, including the possibility of 20 eligible refreshes over time if both actions cost 50. Domain cooldowns limit bursts. They do not erase the outstanding liability. Do not lower published odds, confiscate balances or assume an arbitrary cap on ads to make the economics balance.

Measure net publisher revenue per verified completion, including no-fill and unearned traffic costs. Measure refresh cost including browser pages and consent scenarios, proxy traffic, model tokens, failed attempts and storage. Measure cached unlock cost separately. To retain an illustrative 30% reserve, require:

    (50.2513 / 50) × [q × refresh cost + (1 − q) × cached unlock cost] ≤ 0.70 × net revenue per completion

Here `q` is the share of redeemed 50-point units that fund refreshes. The formula conservatively budgets full redemption and the highest expected points issuance in the model. It includes allocation of a guaranteed guest unlock as one cached unit. Game hosting and other variable overhead belong on the cost side if material.

**Illustration, using assumed prices.** At €0.010 net per completion, €0.025 per refresh and €0.0003 per cached unlock, the funded refresh share is about 27%. If everyone redeems for refreshes, the same reserve needs roughly €0.036 net per completion. Giving those points a credible durable value requires reserving for outstanding balances. Until live costs and demand support the 50-point refresh, run it with test inventory and a limited experiment budget. For real inventory offer the proven domain unlock, or price refreshes separately from cached reads before issuance. For example, at those assumed costs, a price of **180 points per refresh** would cover an all-refresh redemption mix at the stated reserve in expectation. Recalculate after adding multi-page scans. No live price or acceptance rate has been verified.

## Reward delivery

Before each ad, identify the exact selected action, the beneficiary and the time window. For a flight offer, use copy such as “Complete one ad for one flight. Collect before it ends to earn 1–1,000 report points. A missed flight earns 0.” Link the full odds formula and examples, with the guaranteed option equally accessible before opt-in. Keep the normal free visit usable after a decline or skipped ad. Rewards remain usable only by the earning visitor inside DataDeal. They have no transfer, cash, resale, API-credit or subscription conversion. Google's rules permit conditional random rewards with prior disclosure of all possible outcomes including zero. This design proposal does not establish publisher approval. [Google rewarded-ad policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9121589?hl=en)

Bind the selected offer and its terms to one server-side ad intent. Verify completion, issue exactly one entitlement or flight ticket, and bank once even across reconnects or multiple tabs. The promised flight can be resumed if the page closes after a grant. After launch, a disconnect does not redraw the endpoint. Preselected targets settle on the server. Report unavailable inventory before offering a reward. Keep production settlement gated on verifying authentic callbacks, completion IDs and recipient binding in the configured web inventory. [Google Ad Manager rewarded web setup](https://support.google.com/admanager/answer/9116812?hl=en)
